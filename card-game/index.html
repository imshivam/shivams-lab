    Mechanics

    Gestures worth building

    Each one below works in the deck above. The dot lights up when you trigger it. Verdicts are my recommendation; the prototype on your phone decides.

    Shake to shuffle

    A sharp spike in acceleration shuffles the deck. Cards jostle, a riffle sound plays, light haptic ticks follow.

    Seen in: Shake to Undo on iOS, every dice app. Web threshold ~15 m/s², 1 s cooldown.

    Keep

    Swipe to draw

    Drag the top card off the stack. It follows your finger one to one, then springs into place.

    Seen in: Pokémon TCG Pocket's swipe to tear a pack. Apple's "follows you frame by frame" principle.

    Keep

    Tap to flip

    The card turns over with a spring and a soft thud. Three questions are revealed together.

    Seen in: Balatro's springy card flips.

    Keep

    Tilt for shine

    The card leans as you tilt the phone and a highlight slides across it, like a foil card or a Wallet pass.

    Seen in: Apple Wallet, TCG Pocket foils, Balatro's parallax. Native: Core Motion + a Metal shader.

    Keep, subtle

    Pass across the table

    "Pass it on" spins the card 180° so the person opposite can read it, and counts who has answered.

    The heart of the original game: both people answer the same question.

    Keep

    Face down to talk

    Put the phone face down and the screen goes dark: you're talking now. Pick it up for the next card.

    Seen in: Heads Up's "phone is part of the group". Native: UIDevice.orientation.isFlat.

    Try

    Blow to draw

    Blow on the phone and the top card lifts off. Uses the microphone level, nothing is recorded.

    Seen in: Smule Ocarina (blow into the mic to play).

    Try

    Sound and haptics together

    Riffle, thud, click: each moment gets a matched sound and tap. Haptics are weak on the web; the app gets Core Haptics.

    Seen in: Apple's HapticRicochet sample, TCG Pocket's sound-led pack opening.

    Keep

    Point to pass

    Point your iPhone at someone's iPhone to hand them the card. Ultra Wideband gives direction and distance.

    Seen in: NameDrop, AirTag Precision Finding. Native only (Nearby Interaction).

    Later, app only

    Room code for remote

    One person starts a room, everyone joins with a code on their own screen and sees the same card.

    Seen in: Jackbox (jackbox.tv), SharePlay on FaceTime.

    Later, phase 5
    In the wild

    What good looks like out there

    Conversation apps today are lists of questions behind a swipe. The games people love make the phone a physical object. Nobody has put the two together.

    A Little More Conversation

    Gamely · card box · £12.99

    Retailers list five themed rounds and three questions per card, 300+ questions. Rounds I could confirm: Warm Up, What If, Back In The Day.

    Steal: three questions per card, so the holder picks how deep to go.

    We're Not Really Strangers

    Card game + apps

    Three levels that deepen: Perception, Connection, Reflection. Wildcards are quick fun tasks between heavy questions. A final card to close.

    Steal: levels that escalate, wildcards as breathers, a closing ritual.

    Where Should We Begin?

    Esther Perel · story cards

    Prompts ask for stories, not answers. Perel's tip: keep each story to a few minutes. Reviewers say some cards go too intense too soon.

    Steal: "tell a story about…" prompts. Avoid: depth before trust.

    TableTopics: The App

    iPhone · freemium

    The obvious version: 50+ free questions, 400+ paid, save favourites.

    Lesson: correct but no feel. This is the baseline to beat.

    Heads Up!

    Ellen · 2013 · iPhone

    Phone on your forehead, tilt down for correct, up to pass. The camera records the room. Played over a billion times.

    Steal: one clear physical gesture that turns the phone toward the group.

    Smule Ocarina

    iPhone · 2008

    Blow into the mic, cover holes on screen, tilt for vibrato. The phone becomes the instrument.

    Steal: the phone becomes the object, not a picture of it.

    Pokémon TCG Pocket

    iPhone, Android · 2024

    Swipe to tear a pack, cards revealed one by one, rare cards glow and crack. Sound design is called the key. Players complain they can't skip it.

    Steal: ceremony on the draw. Avoid: ceremony you can't speed past.

    Balatro

    Mac, iPhone, consoles · 2024

    Cards float, tilt toward you, flip with a spring; foil has parallax; shake scales with the score. Many small feedback layers stacked.

    Steal: stacked feedback on a short loop.

    Jackbox Party Pack

    TV + phones

    Everyone joins at jackbox.tv with a room code. No controllers. Spectators can join as an audience.

    Steal: the room-code join for the remote and team version.

    Donut

    Slack, Zoom

    Pairs teammates for short chats and posts icebreaker questions, including mid-call on Zoom.

    Lesson: teams already want icebreakers; theirs are plain text prompts.

    Sources: Gamely, The Book Nook, WNRS, Mechanics of Magic, Esther Perel, TableTopics, MacRumors, Macworld, GoNintendo, Sportskeeda, Balatro guide (secondary), Jackbox, Donut.

    Principles

    Rules for a conversation game

    1. Go deeper slowly, and let people choose

      Aron's 1997 "36 questions" study built closeness between strangers with three sets that get more personal. His advice: don't share too much, too fast. Decks go light to deep; three questions per card let the holder choose.

    2. Both people answer

      Closeness comes from taking turns to share, and from responding to what the other person said. Passing the same question on is the default, not an option.

    3. Always a way out

      The main complaint about Perel's game is cards that get intense too early. Offer another of the three, a skip, or a pass.

    4. Breathers

      WNRS wildcards break heavy questions with a quick, fun task. One every six cards or so keeps the energy up.

    5. Eyes up, not down

      Heads Up works because the phone faces the group. Show the question large, then get out of the way: dim, or go face down.

    6. Ceremony, but fast

      Shuffle and draw should feel great the first time and still be quick the fiftieth. TCG Pocket players resent animations they can't skip.

    7. Picture, sound and touch together

      Every important moment gets all three at once. Routine actions stay quiet; too much "juice" tires people out.

    8. No sensor is required

      Every gesture has a tap twin. Reduce Motion turns off shake and tilt. VoiceOver reads the card.

    Sources: Elaine Aron in Psychology Today, A More Beautiful Question, Game feel, Jonasson & Purho, Juice it or lose it (2012), WWDC21 audio-haptic design.
