# Card game: research

30 October 2026. Everything below was checked against a source, listed at the end of each section. Where a source is secondary or I could not confirm something, it says so.

## 1. The original: *A Little More Conversation* (Gamely)

- A pocket-sized box of question cards. Retailers describe **five themed rounds** and **a choice of three questions on every card**, "over 300 questions", from quick icebreakers to deep ones. £12.99.
- Round names I could confirm: **Warm Up** (quick and playful), **What If** (hypotheticals), **Back In The Day**. I could not find the other two names online. Shivam remembers four categories; worth checking the box.
- Marketed for "friends, family, partners, colleagues, or strangers".
- The choice of three is the clever part: the person holding the card controls how deep to go, so nobody is forced into a question they don't want.

Sources: [Gamely](https://gamelygames.com/products/a-little-more-conversation), [John Lewis](https://www.johnlewis.com/gamely-a-little-more-conversation-game/p5677814), [The Book Nook](https://booknookuk.com/product/gamely-a-little-more-conversation/), [McGrocer](https://mcgrocer.com/products/gamely-a-little-more-conversation-question-cards).

## 2. What else is out there

### Conversation games (physical)

| Game | Structure worth stealing |
|---|---|
| **We're Not Really Strangers** | Three levels that get deeper: Perception (first impressions of each other), Connection, Reflection. Wildcards are short, fun tasks that give a break from heavy questions. A final card each to close. 150 questions, 2–6 players. |
| **Where Should We Begin?** (Esther Perel) | Story cards, not questions: "tell a story about…". Optional prompt cards and tokens. Perel's tip: keep each story to a few minutes. Reviewers say some cards get intense too early. |

### Closest rival: Openers

[openers.app](https://openers.app/), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/openers-question-card-games/id6451129455). Shivam found it on 31 Oct; my first research missed it.

- **What it is:** "The question card game that only works in person." One phone sits on the table. Someone draws, someone asks, and everyone answers. Tap to deal the next card. Turn the phone sideways and the question fills the screen for the table.
- **Content:** 60+ decks (Date Night, Team Bonding, Long Distance, Road Tripping and more) and 10,000+ hand-written questions. Each deck has three depths: fun, casual and deep. You choose how far tonight goes.
- **Extras:**
  - an AI tool writes a 30-card deck for an occasion, and you review each card
  - a video answer recorded with the question on screen
  - offline play, no account needed, and 11 languages
- **Business:** free to start, then a subscription or a one-off lifetime purchase.
- **Look:** two-colour illustrations of people around a table, lowercase text, and named colour themes like "red rock" and "lavender rain". It's the only app here with a strong look of its own. It belongs with our printed and tactile direction.
- **Traction:** one indie developer, out since December 2024, with 23 ratings in the US. It ranks well on Google but is still small.
- **What it does that we planned:** in-person play, one phone, choosing the depth, decks by occasion, team bonding.
- **What it doesn't do, from its site and store page:**
  - three questions per card, where the holder picks one
  - the next person answers too
  - passing the card
  - shake, flip, tilt or face down
  - remote play on the same card
- **What it means:** the idea of "a question app for the table" is taken. The parts still open are the ones that make the phone feel like a card: the choice of three, the pass, the physical feel, and remote play. That is where our effort should go. Shivam should play Openers once before we design anything.

### Games that use the phone's body (the feel we want)

| Example | Mechanic | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **Heads Up!** (Ellen, by Impending, 2013) | Phone held to your forehead. Tilt down for correct, tilt up to pass. The front camera records the clue-givers. | One clear physical gesture made the phone part of the group, not a screen people stare at. Played over a billion times. |
| **Smule Ocarina** (2008) | Blow into the mic, cover four "holes" on screen, tilt for vibrato. | Turning the phone into the object itself is what people remember. |
| **Pokémon TCG Pocket** (2024) | Swipe to tear open a pack, cards revealed one at a time, rare cards glow with a crack sound and colour change. | Ceremony makes a draw feel special. But players complain when they can't skip it: ceremony must be fast or skippable after the first time. |
| **Balatro** (2024) | Cards float and tilt toward the cursor, flip with a spring, foil layers have parallax, screen shake scales with the score. | Many small feedback channels stacked together. The game works because the core loop is short and punchy. |
| **Apple's HapticRicochet** (WWDC21 sample) | Tap a ball, tilt the phone, every hit has matched sound + haptic + picture. | Apple's own reference for "picture, sound and haptic as one". |
| **NameDrop, Precision Finding** (iOS) | Ripple from where phones touch; haptics grow as you get closer. | The phone's physical position becomes the interface. |

## 3. Principles for a conversation game

1. **Escalate slowly, and let people choose depth.** Aron's 1997 "36 questions" study made strangers feel closer with three sets of questions that get more personal; Aron's summary was don't share too much, too fast. WNRS uses three levels. The original's three-questions-per-card lets the holder choose. We should keep both: decks ordered light → deep, and three choices per card.
2. **Both people answer.** Closeness comes from back-and-forth self-disclosure, and later research (Reis) adds being responsive to what the other person shares. "Pass it on and they answer too" is the core of this game. The app should make the second answer the default, not an option.
3. **Always a way out.** Cards that feel too intense too early are the main complaint about Esther Perel's game. Offer: pick another of the three, skip the card, or a "pass" token.
4. **Breathers.** WNRS wildcards break up heavy questions with quick fun tasks. A wildcard every ~6 cards keeps energy up.
5. **The phone should bring people together, not pull eyes down.** Heads Up works because the phone faces the group and the gesture is the game. Our screen should be readable from across a table, with very little to read besides the question, then get out of the way while people talk (dim, or go face down).
6. **Ceremony, but fast.** Shuffle and draw should feel great the first time and still be quick the fiftieth (TCG Pocket lesson).
7. **Stack feedback, keep the loop short.** Picture, sound and haptic together on every important moment (Balatro, Apple's audio-haptic guidance). Restraint on routine actions; over-juicing tires people out.
8. **Never needs a sensor.** Every motion gesture has a tap or button twin. Reduce Motion turns off shake and parallax.

Sources: [Psychology Today, Elaine Aron](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-highly-sensitive-person/201501/36-questions-for-intimacy-back-story), [A More Beautiful Question](https://amorebeautifulquestion.com/36-questions/), [Game feel (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_feel), Jonasson & Purho, *Juice it or lose it* (Nordic Game Jam, 2012).

## 4. Sensors and mechanics on the iPhone

| Sensor / API | What it can do in the game | iPhone app | Web (Safari) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accelerometer** (Core Motion) | Shake to shuffle. Detect a sharp spike in acceleration, with a short cooldown. | Yes, also the built-in `motionEnded(.motionShake)` | Yes, `devicemotion`, after `DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission()` from a tap, HTTPS only. Typical threshold ~15 m/s², ~1 s cooldown |
| **Gyroscope / attitude** (Core Motion) | Tilt the deck: cards lean, foil and light shift (Wallet-style shine), parallax. Tilt to choose between the three questions. | Yes, `CMMotionManager` attitude | Yes, `deviceorientation` (same permission) |
| **Face up / face down** | Put the phone face down on the table = "we're talking now", screen goes dark. Pick it up = next. Flip the phone to flip the card. | `UIDevice.orientation.isFlat`, gravity vector | Gravity vector from `devicemotion` |
| **Proximity sensor** | Covered = face down or in a pocket. | `isProximityMonitoringEnabled` (not all devices) | No |
| **Haptics** (Core Haptics) | Riffle on shuffle, thud on draw, click per question, a heartbeat while someone answers. Designed with matched audio. | Full control | Not really: no Vibration API on iPhone. A hidden switch-checkbox trick gives one tick, reportedly patched in iOS 26.5 (unconfirmed). Android has `navigator.vibrate` |
| **Microphone** | Blow to blow the card off the stack, or detect when the room goes quiet / gets loud. | AVAudioEngine level metering, needs permission | `getUserMedia`, needs permission |
| **Touch** | Drag, flick with momentum, long-press to peek, swipe to pass. | SwiftUI gestures | Pointer events |
| **Camera / face** | Heads Up-style recording of reactions. Probably not for a gentle game. | ARKit / AVFoundation | getUserMedia |
| **UWB** (Nearby Interaction) | Point your phone at someone to pass them the card. iPhone 11+. | Yes, permission needed | No |
| **Nearby / multi-device** | Each person on their own phone in the same room. | MultipeerConnectivity | WebSocket room with a code |
| **SharePlay** | Same card on everyone's screen in a FaceTime call. | GroupActivities | No (use a web room) |

Notes:
- Motion sensors don't work in the iOS Simulator; testing needs a real iPhone.
- iOS apps using motion must state why in `NSMotionUsageDescription`.
- Holographic tilt on cards: read attitude from Core Motion, feed it to a SwiftUI Metal shader (`[[stitchable]]`). ShaderKit is an open-source starting point.

Sources: [Core Haptics, WWDC19](https://wwdcnotes.com/notes/wwdc19/520), [Practice audio haptic design, WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10278), [Shake detection on the web](https://slicker.me/javascript/shake/shake.htm), [shake-detector](https://github.com/serglider/shake-detector), [ios-haptics](https://npmjs.com/package/ios-haptics), [WebKit bug 285120](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=285120), [UIDevice proximity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uidevice/isproximitymonitoringenabled.md), [Nearby Interaction](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/nearbyinteraction.md), [ShaderKit](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/jamesrochabrun/shaderkit), [Tilt and flip card in SwiftUI](https://zenn.dev/touyou/articles/flip_tilt_card_swiftui).

## 5. Recommendation

**Keep:** shake to shuffle, swipe to draw, tap or turn to flip, tilt for shine (subtle), phone face down while talking, haptics + sound on shuffle, draw, flip and pass.
**Try in the prototype, then decide:** tilt to choose a question, blow to draw, point (UWB) to pass.
**Skip for now:** camera, proximity.

The deciding test is phase 1: a web prototype on Shivam's iPhone. If the deck doesn't feel physical with shake, draw and flip alone, no extra sensor will save it.
